# ADR-0030 · Streamlines through the volume

**Status:** accepted · 2026-11-04 · amended 2026-11-04 (amendment 1: inlet seeds, volume vectors) · relies on ADR-0026 (Results surfaces, the agent's cuts), ADR-0029 (the agent's allow-list)

## Context

The Stream look traced lines only on the run's mid-plane slice (`run.streamlines`, patch `slice`),
because the first tracer was built under a constraint of no agent change and no volume field. That
limits the picture: a line walks off a slice as soon as the flow has a component across it (measured:
10 mm on the audit's duct), and on the Flanged pipe the lines stopped after 7 cm of a 49 cm pipe
because VTK's default of 2,000 steps ended them (`step` was documented as a fraction of a cell and
implemented as metres). The agent already reads a solved case's internal mesh with `vtkOpenFOAMReader`
for the slices and the iso-surfaces, so the volume is available where VTK and the case already live.

## Decision

1. **Volume tracing is an agent op, `stream.trace`**, using `vtkStreamTracer` (BSD) on the internal mesh
   with the reader's cell-to-point `U`. It is allow-listed like `iso.cut` and answers in the reply
   (at most 200 lines of 1,000 points as float32 bytes), not as an artifact. Module:
   `agent/stokes_agent/trace.py`; the integrator part (`trace_mesh`) takes any VTK dataset, so it is
   tested in memory.
2. **Integration:** adaptive Runge-Kutta 4-5; the step is in **cells** (initial 0.2, minimum 0.01,
   maximum 0.5; VTK's `CELL_LENGTH_UNIT`, which is 2); the maximum length is in **metres** (default
   three times the mesh's diagonal); at most 20,000 steps per line; a line slower than 1e-4 of the
   case's largest speed stops (stagnation).
3. **Every line says why it stopped**: `left_domain`, `length`, `steps`, `stagnation`.
4. **Long lines are resampled by index (ends kept), never refused.**
5. **Seeds are generated by pure functions** in `stokes.post.seeds`: a line (rake), a plane anywhere, a
   sphere, and a patch (area-weighted, moved into the fluid), deterministic, at most 200.
6. **The surface tracer stays** for "on this cut" (`stokes.post.streamlines`), fixed to the same step
   cap and integrator, with speeds taken from the tracer's own interpolation.
7. **Next (not in this ADR's first commit):** `run.streamlines` takes a `source` (volume | slice |
   patch) and a seeds spec; the client draws depth-tested lines that follow the section; the Stream
   look stops forcing the mid-plane slice on.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|
| Keep tracing on the slice | Lines leave the plane at once whenever the flow crosses it |
| Trace in the Python core from a cached cell field | The cache holds patch and slice values, not the volume; a second cache for it would duplicate the reader |
| Return the lines as an artifact like the slice | At most a few MB; a fetch and a cache name buy nothing |
| Fixed step in metres | A mesh from 1 mm to 10 cm cells has no one good step |

## Consequences

Streamlines can start anywhere in the fluid and run the length of the model. The agent gains one op,
so its allow-list and the protocol docs list it. A host without VTK cannot trace (the same as the
slices). Budgets to add to `docs/engineering/performance.md` once measured: 200 lines on a 1 M cell
mesh under 2 s warm.

## Amendment 1 (2026-11-04): seeds on the inlets, vectors through the volume, no slice under either

The owner's report: *"the stream lines and vectors are not correct and not volumetric"*. Root causes,
each read from the code and measured where it could be:

1. **The volume lines were drawn as a picture on one plane.** The Stream look still turned the mid-plane
   slice on (decision 7 was not done), and the slice's cut sections the walls at that plane through the
   renderer's global clipping planes, which `LineMaterial` honours: every line on the near side was
   clipped away with the walls. The rest were drawn with `depthTest: false`, so lines behind the slice and
   behind each other were painted over it. Arrows were drawn the same way.
2. **The seeds missed the fluid.** Line, Plane and Sphere seeds are laid over the model's bounding *box* on
   the mid-plane. On an L-shaped model (a mixing elbow) most of that box is not fluid: measured on an
   in-memory L duct, a 20-seed row 5 % into the box started 7 lines; the Inlet kind took the first inlet
   only, and only the row where it met the plane. A seed on a boundary face started no line.
3. **Vector arrows were not a vector plot.** They were the fastest N samples of the slice (all of them in a
   jet's core, none in the slow stream), from the first group of the cut only, and an arrow's length was
   its place in the min..max range floored at 15 %, not its speed.

Decisions:

1. **Inlet seeds by default, spread by the host.** `stream.trace` takes `seed_patches` (boundary patch
   names) and `seed_count`: the agent reads the patches from the case, spreads the seeds evenly by patch and
   by area within one, and moves every seed into the fluid (`into_domain`: a third of the way from the face
   to the nearest cell's centre, checked inside; a seed farther from the mesh than its nearest cell's size is
   dropped). `run.streamlines` passes them through; the client names every inlet's patch. Measured on a solved
   pipe (97 k cells, snappyHexMesh polyhedra): 50 seeds on the inlet, 50 lines, all `left_domain` at the
   outlet, 2.0 s; the elbow is in the table below.
2. **Vectors through the volume are an agent op, `vector.sample`** (`stokes_agent.sample`): a regular lattice
   sized so about `count` points land in the fluid (a first probe measures the fluid's share of the box), points
   outside dropped, the reader's cell-to-point `U` at the rest (the field the lines follow), interpolated with the
   containing cell's own weights found through one `vtkStaticCellLocator` (BSD). `vtkProbeFilter` gave the same
   answer 38 times slower on polyhedra (3.8 s for 5,115 points). At most 2,000. `run.vectors` answers
   `{points, vectors, spacing}`.
3. **Neither Stream nor volume Vectors stands on a slice.** Both take the slices off and fade the walls
   (`wallsFaded`'s `inside`); lines and arrows are depth-tested and write depth, so the faded walls (drawn in
   the transparent pass) tint them without hiding them. Vectors has a Where control (Volume | Slice); a host
   that cannot sample falls back to the slice, and a host that cannot trace falls back to the slice trace,
   both as before.
4. **Slice arrows are spread evenly over every group of the cut** (one per occupied cell of the coarsest
   grid that fills the asked count), and **every arrow's length is `|U| / max |U|`** of a full arrow.

Measured on the solved mixing elbow (77 k cells; large inlet at x = -0.2 m, small inlet at y = -0.225 m,
outlet at y = +0.2 m, symmetry plane z = 0), read only:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Stream, default seeds | Line: 0 of 50 seeds in the fluid (the row at y = -0.204 m across the box, at its mid-height, misses both pipes), 0 lines | Inlet: 50 seeds (25 per inlet), 50 lines, all `left_domain`, 49 ending at the outlet, 2.6 s |
| Line points inside a cell | - | 7,258 of 7,258; direction cosine of each step with the cell's `U`: median 0.99995, none negative |
| Volume vectors | - | 400 of 400 asked, all inside a cell, 3 layers through z, cosine with the cell's `U` median 0.99996, 1.3 s including the read |

`FindClosestPoint` on a polyhedral cell (VTK 9.6, snappyHexMesh) names the right cell but answers garbage
for its point and -1 for the distance, so `into_domain` takes only the cell id from it.
